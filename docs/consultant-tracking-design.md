## 1. What Consultant Tracking is, and is not

Consultant Tracking **owns the commercial record of consulting work**: who the clients are, what was agreed with each
(the engagement — hourly plus expenses, or a fixed fee against milestones), who worked how long on what, what was spent
on the client's behalf (a receipt behind every expense), what the client's AI systems cost to host and run (tokens,
API calls, VMs — the costs that make AI consulting different), and what of all that has been billed, sent, viewed and
paid. It is the Harvest, Productive or BigTime a small consultancy would otherwise pay for, with the differences that
matter here:

- **It is the one place consulting work becomes money, and it is not the books.** The kernel decided the accounting
  system is an optional application (General Ledger); this application **bills** and the ledger **accounts**. An
  invoice issued here is a document with lines and a backing detail; what it posts to is the ledger's business, read
  from this application's shares through the kernel (§8). Nothing here is a journal, an account or a bank line.
- **Every billable thing is billed once, or never.** A time entry, an expense, a usage charge and a milestone each
  land on at most one invoice line (the database refuses a second); what is approved and unbilled is the WIP the
  firm is owed, and it is a question with one answer (§7).
- **AI costs are first-class billable items.** A `usage_charge` is a quantity of a thing (tokens, API calls, GB,
  VM-hours, a month of hosting) at a cost, billed at cost, at cost plus a markup, or at a resale price per unit —
  entered by hand, imported from a provider's usage export, generated by a hosting schedule, or **allocated from the
  kernel's own closed monthly AI statement** by rules the firm writes (agent → engagement). A token budget on an
  engagement warns before it is blown.
- **A receipt is evidence, not decoration.** An expense above the receipt threshold cannot be submitted without one;
  the phone's camera is the first way in; the receipt travels with the invoice's backing detail when the client is
  owed it.
- **Agents draft; a person approves, issues, sends and writes off.** The shipped Billing Clerk nudges, drafts the
  period's invoices, allocates the AI statement, flags budgets and chases — and issues nothing, sends nothing and
  forgives nothing without a person (§5).
- **Full MCP coverage is a requirement**, not a feature: every report a screen shows is a records tool, every button
  an action tool (`maludb-os-integration`, `mcp-and-api.md`).
- **Nothing of identity lives here.** The kernel signs people in; the application mirrors the directory. A client is
  a party, not a member; an invoice reaches a client by email and a secure link, never a login (§4).

Not in version 1: retainers (prepaid hours or a monthly fee drawn down — the schema admits the kind, the build does
not); quotes and proposals; a client portal with a login (the secure link is the client's door; a magic-link "my
invoices" page is Extended); online payment on the link (Stripe is in the catalog; a provider needs the kernel's secret
store); a PDF renderer (an invoice is a print-ready page in v1, as General Ledger decided); multi-currency conversion
(each client keeps its currency, nothing is converted); receipt reading by a model (no model is ever called from PHP;
an expert-assisted "read this receipt" is Extended through the chat endpoint when the kernel's chat carries images);
resource scheduling and forecasting; payroll (HR's); the ledger itself (General Ledger's). Each is an Extended item (§11).

## 2. Who uses it (the actors, in the OS's words)

**A word about words.** "Agent" always means an OS AI agent. The person who does the work is a **consultant**; the
person who owns an engagement and approves its time and expenses is its **manager**; the person who issues invoices is
**billing**; the outside accountant is **finance**, a kernel member flagged external, admitted by a grant. A **client**
is a party in this application, never a member, never signed in. An **engagement** is the commercial agreement with a
client (a statement of work); a **project** is Projects' word and lives there.

| Actor | Who | How they reach the application |
|---|---|---|
| **Consultant** | Logs time and expenses on the engagements they are staffed on; sees their own timesheets, expenses, reimbursements and the engagements' public facts (client, dates, budget burn) | The launcher → `consulting.<domain>`; the phone (375 px first); the command bar |
| **Manager** | Owns engagements: staffs them, sets rates within the card, approves time and expenses, enters and approves usage charges, watches budgets, drafts invoices for billing | The application, the command bar |
| **Billing** | Clients and their contacts, rate cards, invoices (draft → issue → send → remind), credit notes, payments recorded, write-offs, exports; sees every engagement | The application |
| **Finance** (external) | Reads everything, downloads every export, sees cost rates and margin; a kernel member with `is_external`, granted by the super-admin (the kernel's db/159 opens the launcher to them) | The launcher, like an employee |
| **CT admin** | Settings, consulting roles, rate cards, cost rates, expense categories, providers, allocation rules, numbering, agents' settings. A super-admin holds it (the kernel gives them the admin role) | The settings area |
| **A client** | A party the firm bills | Email, and the invoice's **secure link** `/i/<token>` (the invoice, its backing detail — hours by day and person, expenses with receipts, usage by provider —, "I paid this on …") |
| **An agent billing** | The shipped **expert** and **Billing Clerk** agents, and any agent a super-admin grants a role | Records MCP (run token) · Actions MCP (relayed) · a duty (§5) |
| **The kernel** | Signs people in, delivers the directory, hires and runs the agents, pauses their actions for approval, serves the month's AI statement, answers the command bar, carries texts (K6) and the ledger's reads (K7) | — |
| **System** | The weekly nudges, budget alerts, invoices falling due on an engagement's cadence, the AI statement poll, hosting schedules, the outbox — the one timer worker | `source = 'cron'`, every row logged |

Tenancy: the firm running the application is the tenant — one installation, one database, one memory with every other
application's episodes. One installation is one firm, one base currency for its costs; clients keep their own currency
on their invoices (§3, scope).

## 3. Who sees what — the rules, in one place

**Roles** (published by `app_roles`, `os.app-roles/1`; exactly one `is_admin`; ordered as shown). The base role's key
is `user` on purpose: the installer's `--grant-standing-departments` gives standing departments the role keyed
`member`, `user` or `write`, and in a consultancy **every insider logs time**, so the switch is right here (unlike
General Ledger, §13.3).

| Role key | Name | Capability | Rights |
|---|---|---|---|
| `user` | Consultant | write | `time.write` (own), `expenses.write` (own), `engagements.view` (staffed), `attachments.write` (own records), `reports.view_own` |
| `manager` | Manager | write | Consultant's + `engagements.manage` (theirs and their departments'), `staffing.write`, `time.approve`, `expenses.approve`, `usage.write`, `usage.approve`, `milestones.write`, `rates.view` (bill rates), `cost.view` (cost and margin **on their engagements**), `invoices.draft`, `reports.view` (their engagements) |
| `billing` | Billing | write | Manager's across **every** engagement + `clients.manage`, `rate_cards.manage`, `billing.write` (issue, send, remind, credit, record a payment, write off, void), `exports.download` |
| `finance` | Finance | read | every read, `cost.view` everywhere, `exports.download`; **no writes** — an external's role, granted ALONE (never with `user`, which logs time) |
| `admin` | CT admin (is_admin) | admin | every right + `settings.manage` (settings, consulting roles, cost rates, categories, providers, allocation rules, numbering, agents' settings) |

**The rules** (`ct_has_right(right)` and `ct_can_see_engagement(engagement_id)` in SQL for the views and in PHP for
the handlers and the menu; caller checks as uncorrelated sets tested once per statement — the kernel's db/160 lesson):

- **An engagement is seen by its staff, its manager, the admins of its owning department, and anyone holding Billing,
  Finance or admin.** `engagement_members` (who is staffed, from when to when) is the one staffing table; a consultant
  taken off an engagement keeps reading their own entries on it and may log no more.
- **Time and expenses are the person's own until submitted.** A consultant reads and edits their own drafts; a
  manager reads everything on the engagements they may see; nobody approves their own time or expenses except a
  super-admin (the retired time module's rule, kept).
- **Cost rates are the most sensitive thing here** (they reveal pay): `member_cost_rates` is read only under
  `cost.view`, and a manager's `cost.view` reaches only the margin of their engagements, never the rate table itself.
  The rate table is the admin's.
- **An approved or invoiced entry is locked** — the trigger refuses every edit for every role; a correction is a
  rejection (back to draft, with a reason) before invoicing, or a credit note after.
- **A billed item is billed once**: `invoice_line_items` is unique per time entry, expense, usage charge and milestone;
  voiding an invoice releases its items (the database does it, in the void).
- **A client sees one invoice** through its secure link: the document, its backing detail as the engagement's
  settings allow (receipt images included only if `share_receipts_with_client`), its payments, its balance; never a
  note, never a cost, never another document. The link is hashed, rotatable and dead N days after the invoice is paid
  (setting; default 180 — General Ledger's D9).
- **An agent sees what its member id may see** — the same functions, no special case; the run-facts gate decides which
  tools it is offered; `is_eval` refuses every write.
- **Scope: none.** One firm. Departments are the firm's **practices** (AI Strategy, ML Engineering, Data — the kernel's
  departments, mirrored) and a dimension on every engagement (`department_id`, the owner) and every time entry (the
  consultant's primary department at the time, for the practice utilization report); never a wall. Sites are not a
  dimension here (consulting is not place-bound).

## 4. Clients without an account — the party and the secure invoice

The kernel's rules: *application users are not OS users*; *never a password, a login form or an account of the
application's own*; for a page someone with no account must reach, **a token is the authority** (Help Desk's D1,
General Ledger's D-A). A client is not a member and must never need to be.

**Recommended: a client is a party; an invoice travels by email with a secure link.**

- `clients` and `client_contacts` are the application's own tables (legal name, short name, billing emails, phone,
  billing address, tax id, payment terms, currency, PO requirement, default rate card, default expense policy, notes,
  `member_id` nullable — a client who was also invited as an external member, e.g. for Help Desk). No login.
- `invoice_send` mails the invoice (MaluMail; the application holds its own key as Help Desk and General Ledger do)
  with the **secure link** `/i/<48 hex>`: a print-ready page (the browser's "Save as PDF" is the PDF in v1), the
  lines, the **backing detail** (time by day and person with the client-visible notes, expenses with their receipts
  when shared, usage by provider and month, milestones accepted), the payments received, the balance due, and one
  button — **"I paid this on … by …"** — which records a *client's claim* (`payment_claims`) for billing to confirm
  when the money shows (through General Ledger's receipts when it is installed, or by hand). Opening the link stamps
  `viewed_at`. Reminders travel the same way. The link never shows a cost, a rate card name, an internal note, or
  another engagement.
- Time-entry notes are two fields: `note` (client-visible, goes on the detail) and `internal_note` (never leaves).
- Public paths — `/i/<token>`, `/i/<token>/attachment/<id>` (a shared receipt), `/api/v1/health`, `/mcp/records`,
  `/mcp/activity` — are named in the vhost's allow-list; nothing else answers without a session.

**Rejected:** inviting clients as members (General Ledger's reasoning; and this application never writes the
directory); a client portal with a login (forbidden by the contract; the magic-link "my invoices" page is Extended on
the help desk's pattern); client approval of timesheets on the link (Extended — a `timesheet_approvals_client` table
and a second secure page; v1 ships the hours as detail on the invoice, which is what most SOWs require).

**The kernel's agents that work for clients.** In an AI consultancy the OS's own agents do client work (through
Projects' tasks, Help Desk's tickets, their own duties). Their cost is tokens, and it appears in the kernel's monthly
statement per **agent, department and application** — never per client (the kernel dropped project attribution in
db/133 and will not carry a client's name). This application attributes it: an **allocation rule** names a kernel
agent, department or application, an engagement, a share (per cent) and a date range; each closed month's lines are
split by the rules into draft usage charges (`source = 'kernel_ledger'`), the unallocated remainder shown on the AI
screen for a person to place or leave as overhead. A finer attribution (a cost tag on a run) is a kernel item for later
(K19, §12).

**Skills** (`skills/`): `consulting-basics` (what a client, an engagement, a timesheet, an expense, a usage charge and
an invoice *are*; which tool answers what; T&M versus fixed bid in one page), `rates-and-billing-rules` (how a rate is
resolved, what is billable, markup and resale, the NTE cap, when an item is locked), `writing-to-clients` (tone; an
invoice email and a reminder, never a threat, never another client's name, never a cost); runbooks (`kind: runbook`):
`monday-missing-time`, `draft-the-period-invoices`, `allocate-the-ai-statement`, `budget-review`.

## 6. Memory model — what Consultant Tracking remembers

**Record memory** (PostgreSQL 17, `<tenant>_consultant_tracking`, db/001–0NN). Money columns are `numeric(14,2)`;
quantities `numeric(18,4)` (tokens are stored as the integer they are and **priced per million** — the unit says how
to read the quantity and the price; never stored "in millions"); unit prices `numeric(14,6)`; the kernel's AI amounts arrive at four decimals and are rounded
to cents **once**, on the usage charge, the remainder on the last charge of an allocation so the month allocates to the
cent. Hours are `numeric(6,2)` in increments of the setting (default 0.25; the retired module kept minutes — this
design keeps hours, the unit every rate card and every client speaks).

## 8. The kernel's part, and the application's doors — the accounting interface

- **Sign-on and the mirror**: the contract (`sign-on-and-directory.md`), the kit copied from Help Desk (the newest
  built). The mirror carries departments as practices.
- **Roles**: `app_roles` on the records server; the kernel's token admitted to it and to the `shares[]` alone.
- **The AI statement** (A5, built): the worker pulls `GET {OS_INTERNAL_URL}/api/v1/ledger/periods.php` and, for each
  **closed** month not yet pulled, `?period=`; kept verbatim; `ai_statement_allocate` splits the lines by the
  allocation rules into draft usage charges per engagement × provider (rounded once, remainder on the last), the
  unmatched lines shown as the month's **unallocated remainder** (overhead, or a rule a person adds then re-allocates
  — a re-allocation voids the month's drafts and makes them again; approved charges stay). An **open** month is shown
  as "not yet closed in the OS" and never allocated.
- **Agents**: run tokens on the two read servers (the run-facts gate, fail closed); the Actions MCP from the registry;
  the approval hook before every action with a category; the chat endpoint for the command bar; the duty for the Clerk.
- **K6 texts**: "Submit your time for last week" (Monday), "Your expense EXP-000123 was approved", "Invoice INV-00042
  was viewed by the client" to billing — when the person chose texts; every refusal falls back to email.
- **The accounting interface is K7 — reads through the kernel, never a write** (the kernel's rule: *no writes between
  applications*). This application is a **provider**; the ledger and HR are its consumers; a super-admin approves each
  connection.

  **Shares (`shares[]`)** — every one a records-server tool, people-free unless marked, each answering a versioned
  document so a consumer never scrapes a screen:
  | Tool | For | Document | Content |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `invoices_issued` | General Ledger (a source import: DR Accounts receivable / CR Service revenue, Reimbursed expenses income, Pass-through income, CR Sales tax payable) | `os.ct-invoices/1` | invoices issued in a period: number, client (name, external ref), engagement number, dates, terms, currency, totals, tax, lines summarised **by kind** (time, expense, usage, milestone, fixed, adjustment), `external_ref` |
  | `credit_notes_issued` | General Ledger | `os.ct-credit-notes/1` | the same shape, negative |
  | `expenses_by_category` | General Ledger (company-card spend by category and month — a draft journal or a bill per card statement) | `os.ct-expenses/1` | approved expenses paid by company card: category, month, amount, tax, billable flag, engagement number; **no person** |
  | `unbilled_summary` | General Ledger (the WIP accrual at month end) | `os.ct-wip/1` | approved unbilled time, expenses, usage and accepted milestones at bill value, per engagement, as of a date |
  | `reimbursements_approved` (`people: true`) | **HR** (pays reimbursements in a pay run; marks them paid) | `os.ct-reimbursements/1` | approved, unreimbursed personal-card expenses per member id: number, date, amount, currency |
  | `engagement_list` | Projects (to link a project to an engagement), Help Desk (to name the client on a ticket) | `os.ct-engagements/1` | active engagements: number, name, client short name, kind, dates; no money |

  receipts (by our invoice number)"* — a share General Ledger owes (G1, §12); until it exists billing records payments

  reminders and statements out, threaded; nothing inbound in v1.
- **Public doors** (allow-listed in the vhost; everything else needs a session): `/i/<token>`,
  `/i/<token>/attachment/<id>`, `/api/v1/health`, `/mcp/records`, `/mcp/activity`. Each is CSRF-protected or
